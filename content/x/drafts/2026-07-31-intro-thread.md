## 1/
Hi — this is my third role at a global tech company, all top firms in their industry, and each one has had me selling into Japan 🇯🇵.

## 2/
Along the way I've worked every stage of the sales process — SDR, BDR, cross-sell AE, and AE across SMB, mid-market, and enterprise. Same country, different companies, very different lessons each time.

## 3/
Japan is a genuinely fascinating, high-potential market for global tech firms — and also stranger and more different than most HQs expect. What works in other regions regularly doesn't work here. I'll share why, concretely, as I go.

## 4/
That's what Genba is: genbajapan.com. I'm currently a full-time enterprise AE, so this is writing and observations, not a service yet — but DMs are open if you're seeing something similar in your own Japan pipeline.

## 5/
A pattern I see a lot: early-adopter traction in Japan looks great at first. Japan's early adopters are genuinely progressive and move fast on new products, so they adopt on their own with little friction.

## 6/
Then you cross the chasm into mid-market and enterprise — and if you don't already know how to fight there, it falls apart fast. The usual culprit is business customs most HQs never see: ringi, consensus-based decision-making, and upward escalation (joshin).

## 7/
Skip preparing your sales process for that at market entry, and here's the spiral: growth suddenly stalls, HQ starts wondering if Japan has no real demand, and pushes down shallow activity-count KPIs. The field sales team burns out, loses motivation, and quits.

## 8/
HQ then asks the Japan country manager or head of sales to explain — who's often fighting a language barrier of their own in that conversation. End result: Japan quietly underperforms, or HQ just decides to pull out.

## 9/
To be clear: global products are often genuinely better than what's built in Japan. I'd love to see them spread here — better productivity, faster growth for the businesses that adopt them.

## 10/
As an AE, my biggest wish is that HQ actually understands how sales works in Japan specifically. When that happens, the Japan sales org can perform at its real ceiling instead of fighting its own headquarters.

## 11/
I do this full-time as an AE, and I want to use X to share the real, on-the-ground (genba) knowledge that comes from actually doing the job — not theory.

## 12/
I'll keep writing it up properly at Genba, the media I run: genbajapan.com. The goal is simple — help more companies actually win in Japan.

## Last/
Follow along, and DM me if you're stuck. I'm heads-down on my day job right now so I can't promise much, but I'll do what I can to help.
