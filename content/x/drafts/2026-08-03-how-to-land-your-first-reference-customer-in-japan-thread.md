## 1/
"Who else here uses this?" — the question every Japanese enterprise prospect asks, and the one your home-market case studies can't answer.

Your first Japanese reference customer isn't deal #1. It's the asset every deal after it depends on.

## 2/
That first customer is absorbing first-mover risk on your behalf. The deal economics should reflect that — outsized exec attention, roadmap input, pricing that wouldn't survive a normal deal-desk review.

## 3/
The mistake isn't making that trade. It's not deciding to make it in advance — and discovering mid-negotiation that the deal only closes if you concede on everything at once, unplanned.

## Last/
Pick your first reference deliberately, not opportunistically. A credible logo in the right industry is worth more concession than a bigger contract nobody in your target market has heard of.
