Foreign retail and e-commerce SaaS companies frequently pitch Japan the same way they pitch every other market: help merchants stand up an independent, brand-owned storefront, and let the product's usual value proposition — control, margin, direct customer relationships — do the talking. In most Western markets that pitch works fine alongside marketplace selling. In Japan, it runs into a structural fact that's easy to underestimate: mall-style marketplaces aren't just a sales channel here, they're a large part of how Japanese consumers establish trust in an unfamiliar merchant in the first place.

## Why the marketplace habit runs deeper than "convenience"

Rakuten's ecosystem — points, cross-shop loyalty, a marketplace-native shopping experience — and Amazon Japan's logistics reliability both do something a standalone DTC site struggles to replicate on its own: they pre-validate the merchant for a buyer who has never heard of the brand. A first-time, brand-unaware Japanese shopper is often more willing to buy from an unfamiliar merchant inside Rakuten than from that same merchant's own site, purely because the marketplace itself is the trusted party.

That means "help merchants go direct-to-consumer and reduce marketplace dependency," a pitch that lands well in the US or Europe, can sound to a Japanese merchant like "help you give up the part of the funnel that's actually doing the trust-building work."

## What this means for the pitch, not just the roadmap

This isn't a reason to avoid the Japanese market — it's a reason to sequence the pitch differently:

- Lead with how the product plugs into or complements the marketplace motion a merchant already runs, before leading with independence from it.
- Reframe "own your customer relationship" around specific, concrete gains (repeat-purchase data, post-purchase CRM, margin on returning customers) rather than as a philosophical alternative to marketplace selling.
- Expect that the merchants most open to a DTC-first pitch are the ones who already have some brand recognition outside marketplaces — inbound-facing brands, or categories where marketplace search doesn't dominate discovery (e.g., higher-consideration purchases).

## The takeaway

A pitch built entirely around disintermediating the marketplace is starting from a position of doubt for a Japanese buyer, because the marketplace is often doing real trust-building work that a standalone storefront hasn't earned yet. The stronger opening move is usually "alongside," not "instead of" — at least until the case for independence is concrete enough for a specific merchant's situation.
