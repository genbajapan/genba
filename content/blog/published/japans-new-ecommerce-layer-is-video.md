We've written before about [why a foreign e-commerce SaaS pitch built around "help merchants go direct-to-consumer" tends to underperform in Japan](/blog/the-mall-effect-ecommerce-in-japan), because the marketplace is doing real trust-building work a standalone storefront hasn't earned yet. A February 2026 announcement is worth revisiting that thesis against: Rakuten and YouTube just launched a shoppable-video partnership, and it's a good test of whether "video commerce" is actually a break from the marketplace-centric pattern, or more of the same thing in a new format.

## What actually launched

Rakuten Group [announced](https://global.rakuten.com/corp/news/press/2026/0219_01.html) that Rakuten Ichiba became the first partner for YouTube's Shopping Affiliate Program in Japan. Creators can tag Rakuten Ichiba products directly inside videos — long-form, Shorts, livestreams, and posts — and viewers tap through to the Rakuten product page to buy. Rakuten is framing it as a push into "v-commerce": video, voice, and eventually VR as new discovery and conversion layers on top of its existing marketplace.

## The pattern this confirms, not breaks

If this were "YouTube shopping bypasses the marketplace," it would complicate the earlier thesis. It doesn't. The purchase still resolves on a Rakuten Ichiba product page — Rakuten's checkout, Rakuten's points ecosystem, Rakuten's post-purchase relationship. Video is a new discovery surface layered on top of the same marketplace-as-trust-anchor structure. If anything, it deepens the pattern: Rakuten is now capturing a social-discovery channel that used to be the strongest argument for going direct-to-consumer (build your own audience on social, sell them directly), and folding it back into the marketplace loop.

## What this means if you're selling into Japanese retail/EC

- **The pitch that says "video/social commerce is your chance to finally go DTC" needs updating.** In Japan, the biggest platform just moved to make video commerce a marketplace feature, not an alternative to one. A merchant's incentive to build independent video-commerce infrastructure outside that ecosystem just got weaker, not stronger.
- **If you sell tooling into this space** — analytics, creator/affiliate management, livestream commerce infrastructure — the near-term buying signal among Japanese retailers is going to be "how do we plug into Rakuten's v-commerce program," not "how do we build our own." Positioning as complementary to that motion will land better than positioning as a replacement for it.
- **Omnichannel research-compare-buy behavior is real and growing** — Japanese consumers research on social, compare on marketplaces, and buy wherever's most convenient in the moment. But "wherever's convenient" is still landing disproportionately on the marketplace checkout, per this move, not on brand-owned storefronts.

## The takeaway

New commerce formats in Japan tend to get absorbed into the existing marketplace-trust structure rather than displacing it — video commerce is the newest example, not an exception. If your pitch depends on this being the year merchants finally go around Rakuten, the market's largest player just made a fairly public bet that the opposite is true.
