We've covered [the entity-vs-agency-vs-fractional decision before](/blog/entity-agency-or-fractional), framed around how much visibility into a deal you're willing to trade for speed. This year's broader channel-strategy content sharpens that further in a way that's directly useful for Japan, and most Japan-specific GTM advice hasn't caught up to it yet.

## The distinction current channel playbooks are making

This year's channel-sales guidance draws a clearer line between partner types than the "just find a local partner" framing most market-entry content uses. Broadly: VARs (value-added resellers) add real services or customization on top of your product and tend to be strongest in enterprise deals; straight resellers buy and resell with little modification and are best suited to geographic expansion where the product doesn't need much local adaptation; and industry consultants or boutique advisory firms carry real C-suite trust but don't want the overhead of running a sales cycle, so a low-friction referral arrangement suits them better than a full reseller agreement.

## Why Japan GTM advice usually skips this

Most "how to enter Japan" content treats channel partner selection as a single decision — find a distributor or local agency, sign them, done. That collapses three genuinely different partner types into one category, and Japan is arguably the market where getting the type wrong costs the most. A straight reseller — good at moving product across a language and logistics barrier with minimal customization — is a fine fit if your product genuinely works out of the box in Japan. It's a poor fit if your enterprise SaaS needs meaningful localization, Japanese-language support responsiveness, or integration work, because a reseller isn't built to carry that load, and won't invest in building it for one vendor's product.

## What this means in practice

- **If your product needs real customization or support depth to land with a Japanese enterprise buyer, you need a VAR-type partner, not a reseller** — and you should be evaluating candidates on services capacity and technical depth, not just their distribution network or client list.
- **If your product is closer to plug-and-play, a reseller relationship can work** — but don't expect that partner to solve localization gaps you haven't already addressed; they're built for distribution, not adaptation.
- **If what you actually need is credibility and warm introductions into enterprise accounts, that's a referral relationship, not a channel partnership** — trying to convert an industry advisor into a full reseller usually backfires, because they never wanted to own the sales cycle in the first place, and forcing the structure just creates a partner who under-delivers on both sides of the arrangement.

## The takeaway

"Find a local partner" is not a strategy in Japan — it's a placeholder for a decision you still have to make. Match the partner type to what your product actually needs (distribution, services, or credibility), the same way this year's broader channel-strategy guidance recommends doing everywhere else. Japan doesn't need a different framework here. It needs the existing one actually applied, instead of skipped in favor of "just find someone local."
